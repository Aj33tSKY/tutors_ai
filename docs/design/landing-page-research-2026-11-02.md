# Kindling landing-page design research

Research date: 2 November 2026. Reviewed 18 public AI product pages through their official sites and desktop browser captures, with additional product-section inspection on six. Layouts and hero messages can vary by location, experiment and visit. These are design judgments, not measured conversion results. This research does not change application code.

## Recommended direction

**A spark of understanding, worth keeping.**

Make the conversation → insight → useful memory sequence the visual identity of Kindling. Keep the green tutor, violet student, fluid orbs, Geist typography, crisp card corners and restrained labels already established. The next improvement should connect the whole page to that identity and demonstrate the value of remembering.

The current animation is a strong beginning. It shows an interaction becoming an observation. It does not yet show a student returning to that observation, using their tutor's method, or preparing a useful next question. The headline promises memory; the visual story should deliver it.

## What the current page is missing

### 1. A complete demonstration of memory

The hero currently ends at AI analysis. The most distinctive value is what happens later. Continue one example into a saved note, then a later revision question that draws on the lesson. A static or user-controlled follow-up can make this clear without adding a second constantly moving display.

The current backend prepares structured notes after lessons and grounds revision chat in available lesson excerpts. Present those capabilities faithfully; do not imply an unbuilt real-time coaching or progress-scoring system. Exact clickable video timestamps should only be presented if supported.

### 2. A visual identity across the whole page

The new floating notes and organic waveforms sit alongside older ruled steps, a blue feature block and generic chat panels. The page feels assembled from different design passes.

Carry three motifs through the page: a highlighted phrase, a restrained connector and a useful note. Reuse them in a lesson recap, a revision answer and the next-session preparation. Use the existing flame mark as an occasional accent rather than adding another mascot or decorative AI symbol.

### 3. Human confidence near the first impression

Two abstract orbs can initially read as two AI agents. Keep the orbs, but introduce real tutors nearby: authentic portraits, qualifications and a specific teaching strength. This is especially useful for a tutoring service where the tutor relationship is part of the purchase.

The current tutor catalogue appears much later and can disappear when no eligible tutors load. Human trust should not rely solely on that optional section. Use a real, available tutor introduction or an existing approved story. Do not substitute invented testimonials, institutional endorsements or grade improvements.

### 4. A clearer hierarchy

The headline, exam-board list, second large subject heading, supporting paragraph, two equally prominent calls to action, stats and moving subject strip compete with the hero story.

Keep one headline, one short explanation, the primary tutor CTA and the lesson demonstration. Reduce the visual prominence of tutor recruitment in the student acquisition journey. Exam-board coverage can sit in quiet supporting copy. Operational facts such as recording retention belong near the relevant feature details or FAQ, rather than acting as aspirational proof.

### 5. More intentional motion

Waveforms, orbs, scenario rotation, width changes, annotations, rotating subject text and a moving subject strip all ask for attention. Give motion a hierarchy: the demo tells the story; surrounding content stays largely still.

Use a curated first example with a clear insight. The 32-scenario library provides variety, but randomising the first story can mean the visitor sees an unresolved misconception rather than a completed benefit. Offer a few purposeful story choices and a pause/replay control. Preserve reduced-motion support and a readable static state.

### 6. Tangible outputs and proof

The feature section's generic lesson-review exchange is substantially weaker than the hero. Replace it with an example that resembles the real product: a tutor-editable recap, a precise concept to revisit, and a brief revision answer grounded in the lesson.

Distinguish evidence from service specifications. Subject counts, exam-board coverage and retention are useful facts; they do not establish tutoring quality. Actual tutor explanations, genuine student/parent feedback and specific product outputs can do that.

## Proposed page sequence

1. **The promise.** Keep “Tutoring that remembers every lesson.” Tighten the supporting copy and make the lesson demonstration the visual focus.
2. **The people.** A small, authentic tutor introduction or portrait group immediately establishes that a human is teaching.
3. **The memory.** Continue the same example: what was said, what was recorded, and how it helps later. One artefact persists through the sequence.
4. **The proof.** A large, legible worked example or real-looking lesson recap. Avoid a tiny whole-dashboard screenshot.
5. **The specialists.** Real tutor profiles with clear subject, teaching strengths and transparent next steps.
6. **The practicalities.** Booking, recordings, editable notes and revision, explained briefly in the context of the student's journey.
7. **The decision.** One clear route to find a tutor, with relevant reassurance and supporting details nearby.

Possible supporting copy, to test rather than treat as final: “Learn with a specialist. Return to their explanations, your breakthroughs, and what to practise next.”

## Visual and motion rules

- Retain the exact primary green (`--primary`) and violet student identity. Avoid green surface washes and unrelated accent colours.
- Retain Geist. Create character through scale, whitespace, confident line breaks and composition rather than introducing several fonts.
- Keep the recently sharpened corners. Treat the lesson card as the primary object and AI notes as light secondary artefacts.
- Use neutral surfaces and believable depth. Shadows should explain which layer an object belongs to, rather than decorate every card.
- Prefer one large, legible demonstration to many small feature tiles.
- Let each transition communicate a cause: a phrase is spoken, an observation appears, a note becomes available for later use.
- Keep surrounding text still while the demo is active. Avoid animated headings and a moving marquee competing with the lesson.
- On mobile, show a deliberately edited sequence rather than shrinking a desktop composition until the text becomes too small.
- Preserve accessible contrast and reduced-motion alternatives. Large brand-colour shapes and small body labels need different contrast treatment.

## Recommended first design pass

**Priority 1: hierarchy and coherence.** Simplify hero copy and secondary motion; replace the operational stats treatment with a relevant human/product proof moment; unify the next two sections with the hero's annotation language.

**Priority 2: complete the promise.** Build the lesson → saved insight → later revision sequence. This is the most valuable new piece of visual storytelling.

**Priority 3: own the brand.** Refine the flame/annotation motif, add authentic tutoring imagery and apply the same typography, spacing and surfaces across the page.

**Priority 4: meaningful interaction.** Add a small set of scenario choices and pause/replay. Do not make the visitor operate a complex fake dashboard to understand the product.

## How to judge whether it works

After a five-second view, a prospective student should be able to say that this is human tutoring with useful lesson memory. After watching one short demonstration, they should understand how something said in a lesson helps them revise later. They should also know the next step without needing to read all the feature copy.

Test the direction with students and parents before treating it as a winning formula. Compare comprehension, recall of the differentiator, tutor-profile visits and qualified booking actions. Extra animation alone is not a success metric.

The proposed formula is: **a clear promise + a recognisable human context + one memorable transformation + concrete evidence + a consistent visual world + an obvious next action.**
